# Happy Camper

A WoW: Forever (interface 16001) addon that makes camps social, by Torridon. Quick command: `/hc`.

Found a bug, or have an idea? Send it with Options → Send feedback.
It works with everyone else who has it, so the more people use it, the better it gets: tell your friends!

## Standard and Enhanced

The first time you log in with Happy Camper, it asks how much you'd like to see. You can change it any time in Options (Mode), and it changes at once.

- **Enhanced** is everything below.
- **Standard** is the basics: camps on the map and minimap with their time left, the Camp tab (rest bar, camp name and song, Say and General, the people at your fire), badges, all the camp music and the Music tab, the Handbook, the Campsite Assistant and layer invites. It leaves out:
  - camp details: who set a camp up, its distance, object slots, equipment and quality (pins have no coloured ring), the Camp tab's Has: line, and the buffs in the Campfire nearby notice;
  - Find a camp (the strip under the map);
  - the Camp Builder, its meet-ups and news;
  - the Group tab.

In Standard, other players' camps aren't exact spots: each shows on the world map as a haze of smoke about 200 yards across, and you know the fire is somewhere in it (not always in the middle). Hover the smoke for the camp's name and time left. On the minimap it's a faded pin in the middle of the smoke. Your own camps, and the camp you're at, show as ordinary pins.

Either way your camps and their objects are shared with other players exactly the same, so a Standard player's camps still show up for everyone.

## Features

- **Camp names**: every camp is named after where it is.
  - **Goldshire Campsite:** inside a named area.
  - **West Goldshire Campsite:** within about 250 yards of one.
  - **Northeastern Durotar Campsite:** further out.

  Whoever set up the camp (lit the fire or first reported it) can pick another name in **This camp** on the Camp tab, for its first 15 minutes. The names come in four groups:
  - **This location:** names with the place in them, local specials for that zone or area (e.g. *Goldshire Whisky Club* near Goldshire) and nearby features (*Lakeside Camp* near a lake).
  - **Your professions:** one for each profession you have.
  - **Campsites** and **Painted signs:** always available.

   Every zone in classic and Forever has local specials. The whole list is in `Names.lua`, easy to add to. Nearby areas come from the map's exploration data, so only areas the fire-lighter has discovered count.
- **Camp network**: players with the addon share their camps over a hidden chat channel. A fire becomes a camp once someone with the addon gets camp buffs from it (Camp Benefits), or places an object at it. A bare fire, with no buffs, never goes on the map and nothing about it is sent, except music played at it (see Camp tab). Each camp shows up on everyone's world map at its real coordinates. Hovering it shows how long the fire has left (counting down live) and its **Camp Equipment**: each item and the buff it gives. Equipment comes from objects placed by players with the addon, and from the Camp Benefits buff of anyone with the addon who rests there, which lists every object at the camp, whoever placed it. Each new Camp Benefits read is the camp's whole list, so objects that have burnt out drop off (for everyone), along with their buff in Find a camp. The exception: an object whose stat a class buff on you already gives (a Lodestone while you have Blessing of Might) may be left out of your list, so it isn't taken away; someone without that buff will see whether it's gone. Hovering a camp object at the camp you're at adds it, which also finds the ones Camp Benefits never lists (the bots). **Camps seen in passing**: near a fire (Campfire Nearby) where no camp is known yet, hovering one of its objects puts the camp on the map without resting, with the objects you hover (hover the fire too for its size). It's placed where you stand, so it's only roughly there: it shows grey and faded like camps from chat, with "Seen in passing by <name>", until someone with Happy Camper rests there and reports it properly (your own rest there confirms yours). If someone rests a full minute and gets no Camp Benefits at all, the camp's objects are gone and it drops off the map until someone places a new one. Objects and fires burn out separately, so a new fire starts with no objects until someone rests there. The fire and each object stand 15 minutes from when they were placed; only the fire gives camp buffs, so a camp ends with its fire, even if objects outlast it. A rest begun just before a fire goes out still ends in Camp Benefits, but that doesn't put the dead fire back on the map. Only the player who lit the fire knows its burn time, so their addon shares it; a camp whose fire nobody with the addon saw lit is taken to have gone out 10 minutes after it was last reported. **Camps from players in your zone**: when you log in, and after a few seconds in a zone you enter (each zone at most once every 10 minutes), the addon quietly asks the Happy Camper players in that zone for its burning camps, so you see camps even if whoever reported them has logged off. One of them passes on each camp with its time left; nothing is said in chat, and a camp you already know is never changed by it. **The Camp Tent** gives rested XP once an hour, and for that hour the game lists it in Camp Benefits at every camp, tent or not; so during that hour a Tent line doesn't add a tent to a camp (a camp already known to have one keeps it).
- **Find a camp**: always under the camp bar, in the panel below the map (framed like the map). `/hc search` opens the map to it.
  - Click the buffs you want. Each is shown as the icon of the object that gives it, with the buff name.
  - **Higher tiers**: click a picked buff again to look only for camps with a better object for it: tier 2 or better (Strength: an Anvil or a Master Forge), then tier 3 (a Master Forge), then off. The icon changes to that tier's object, with one, two or three stars on its ring; its tooltip names the objects that count. A camp known to give a buff without its tier known counts as tier 1. Right-click a buff to stop looking for it.
  - Choose how many a camp needs: Any, 2+, ... All. The camp bar above counts the matching camps, with how many of your buffs each needs ("Matching camps: 3 (all 2)").
  - **Quick picks**, the Melee, Caster and Balanced buttons under the buffs, pick the buffs for Melee (Strength, attack power, crit, Stamina, all stats), Caster (mana regen, Intellect, Spirit, crit, Stamina, all stats) or Balanced (Stamina, all stats, armor, crit) in one click.
  - With buffs picked, only camps that match show on the map and the camp bar. **Clear** empties the picks to see every camp again; your picks are kept for next time.
  - Hover a buff to see its value at your level (and, in grey, the most it gives and from which level), which objects give it, and which class buff it doesn't stack with.
- **Before placing a camp object**: its tooltip in your bags (and its Campsite Assistant slot) warns when a class buff you have already gives its stat (others at the camp still get it), when the camp you're at already has that object or a better one, or when its fire is full.
- **Camp links in chat**: clicking a map pin link someone posted shows, in your own chat window, how long that camp's fire has left, or how long ago it went out (for fires seen ending in the last 30 minutes). Nothing is sent.
- **World map**: fire pins for camps and map pins for Camp Builder meet-ups. The camp bar sits just below the map window (or along the bottom of a full-screen map) and shows how many camps are on the map you're viewing, buttons for Show camps, Other layers, the Camp Builder and the options in the middle, with Find a camp always under it, and your camp buffs and object cooldown on the right. Camps show on the map of any zone you look at, wherever you are (a capital city counts as part of the zone around it, so Orgrimmar's camps show on Durotar's map too). Continent and world maps show them too, as **Continent and world maps** in Options says: grouped (the default: one pin per zone, with how many camps it has; click one to open that zone's map), every camp with smaller pins (in Standard mode a small dot of smoke), or none. At most 25 show on a zone's map and 60 on a continent's or the world's (with every camp): nearest you on a map you're on, else nearest the middle of the map you're viewing. Hover a camp's pin for its name, time left, equipment, how many of its object slots are used (Basic fires hold 3, Journeyman 5, Expert 10) and whether it's on your layer. Objects whose buff you already get from a class buff (a Fish Bowl when you have Blessing of Kings, say) are marked in red, since they don't stack; Options can turn that off. **Layers**: the addon reads which layer you're on from NPCs you see (nameplates, mouseover, target). The first one in a zone gives it for now; once two agree it's settled, and then it takes a few NPCs agreeing to change it, so one across a zone border doesn't. Camps on another layer are faded, or hidden with the Other layers button on the camp bar (or "Show camps on other layers" in Options), and once your layer is settled they never count as the camp you're at: your fire beside one is your own camp. A camp in your zone whose layer can't be told says why in its tooltip: its layer isn't known (an older version's camp, or one seen in passing or posted in chat), or yours isn't yet. If you stand within 50 yards of a camp's spot for 5 seconds with no Campfire Nearby (it reaches about 100 yards), its fire isn't on your layer, or has gone out: from then on it's treated as another layer's (faded, or hidden), on your computer only, for the layer you were on, until you're near a fire there again. **Faction**: only your own faction's camps and Camp Builder requests are shown. Clicking a meet-up pin opens the Camp Builder. **Show camps** (the fire icon) hides every camp from the map until you click it again; Camp Builder meet-ups still show. It's also in Options.
- **Controllers**: with the game's own controller support, the game blocks its map and menus (and blames the addon) when an addon's code runs as they open. So nothing of Happy Camper's runs as the map opens, closes or changes size (the camp bar, Find a camp and the pins catch up a moment later), its menus (the Campsite Assistant's slot menus, the dropdowns in Options and the tabs, choosing whom to ask for a layer invite) are plain ones of its own, and its map buttons ask you to open the map yourself instead of opening it. A controller addon's cursor (ConsolePort's) skips everything Happy Camper puts on the world map: the camp bar, Find a camp and the map pins. That cursor runs a frame's own scripts outside the game's protected path, so landing on one of them as the map opened got its own next action blocked and blamed on Happy Camper. The camp bar's settings are all in Options too; with a mouse nothing changes.
- **Ashes and popular campsites**: something on the map between live camps, while there are few. Neither is a camp: each has its own look, says what it is in its tooltip, and never counts as a camp (not in the camp bar's count, Find a camp, or the camp you're at). **Ashes** (a grey campfire): a camp that burns out leaves ashes for 3 hours, with how long ago, its name and (Enhanced) what it had. **Popular campsites** (a soft orange circle, like a fire's glow, in a gold ring): spots where at least two different players have camped in the last 2 weeks (each camp counts for 2 weeks, so a spot fades as camping there stops, and one nobody has camped at for 2 weeks is forgotten): the beta's (a list made from the developer's capture of Happy Camper's own traffic, places only; there were Alliance ones only) and ones your own addon learns as camps end. Their tooltips invite you to light a fire there. A live camp at the spot hides its ashes or campsite. Both are your faction's only, kept on your computer (never sent), and cleared at launch with the beta's other world data. The camp bar's **Recent camps** and **Popular campsites** buttons, beside Show camps and Other layers, turn them on and off (Options too). The camp bar also shows **how many other Happy Campers were seen today** (hover it for this week's; Options can hide it).
  - **Layer invites** (opt-in): to change layer, you need a group invite from someone on the other one. Tick "Let players on other layers ask to join my layer" (Options -> Camps and sharing) and, while you're at a camp, players who see it on another layer can ask you: the camp's faded pin names you, and a click on it asks (a menu picks one if several can be asked). You get a small window, "<name> asks to join your layer.", with Invite and Not now; only Invite sends an invite. It closes by itself after a minute or on Escape. Asks go as a hidden addon whisper, never in chat, and only reach you at that camp, out of a fight and an instance, and not from blocked players or your own group. Each player can ask the same player once every 10 minutes (3 asks a minute at most), and is shown at most once every 10 minutes, one at a time. Off at first.
- **Happy Camper window**: the addon's one window outside the map. Open it with `/hc` or by left-clicking the minimap button. It opens by itself on the Camp tab when you sit at a camp, and closes when you walk away, unless you opened it yourself. It's movable and remembers its position and last tab. It has seven tabs along its bottom, icons in the style of the Looking For Group window's side tabs (hover one for its name):
  - **Camp**:
    - **Your camp buffs** at the top: Camp Benefits with its time left, and a bar that drains over its hour (orange once you'd be warned); a line for each object's buff, with that object's icon (not the Tent line the game keeps at every camp for the hour after a tent's rested XP: a camp has a tent only when that hour began with your rest there, from the game's Boosted Rest); and a row of **every camp buff** as a big icon, spread across the card, lit for the ones you have, blue for one a class buff on you already gives, greyed for the rest. Hover a greyed one for the objects that give it and who at your fire has that profession; click any for the Handbook on it. Under them, when your camp object can be placed again.
    - **This camp** (under Find a camp): the camp you're at. Whoever set it up picks its **name** in its first 15 minutes, and its **song** any time, from two menus of the same size; everyone else sees them. The button at the right of the "This camp" line is **Play**/**Stop** for the owner, and **Mute** (and **Unmute**) for everyone else. Beside it, **Perform** lets anyone at the camp play a found song for everyone there, once every 5 minutes: it plays in place of the camp's song, and performances queue up in the order they come.
      - **Found songs** have to be found before you can play them. Most are Happy Camper's own music, written for it, each found at one place in the world, such as a ranch, a dam or a ruined town: rest at a camp there to learn it. The pirate shanty and the Darkmoon Faire are the game's music, found too (below). Finding one shows a small card at the top of the screen (Song found, its title and where) and plays its first few seconds, just for you; click it for the Music tab (Options → Alerts → New songs on screen turns it off). Songs you find are shared by all your characters, like badges. The Song menu lists the **camp tracks**, the game's looping music (your race's and faction's, and the pirate shanty and Darkmoon Faire once found). Happy Camper's own songs that you've found are your **Repertoire**, played with **Perform**; the Music tab shows them all, with a hint for the rest. Everyone at the camp hears a performed song, found or not.
      - The other songs are the game's own music: a quiet night tune for everyone; the Alliance or Horde tavern for your faction; and your race's own (the human or undead tavern, the dwarven tavern for dwarves and gnomes, Temple of the Moon, the orc rest area for orcs and trolls, the Tauren rest stop). The **pirate shanty** has to be found: rest at a camp in Booty Bay, the Wild Shore or Ratchet. The **Darkmoon Faire** song is found by resting at a camp in Elwynn Forest or Mulgore while the Faire is on (Happy Camper reads the game's calendar when you log in). Playing one emotes *strikes up a familiar tune.*
      - Everyone with Happy Camper at the same camp hears it through their own Music volume; nobody anywhere else does, and players without the addon never see or hear anything. One of the game's songs loops through its tracks for as long as you're at the camp; a Repertoire song plays once, then the camp's own song (the last camp track played there) comes back; any song stops when you leave; anyone arriving later hears it too (the owner's addon sends it to them). The owner can change it at most once a minute. Options can turn off music from others.
      - The song you pick is remembered. It starts by itself, with no emote, at each fire you light and each camp you're the first with Happy Camper to set up. A song started at your bare fire carries on when the fire becomes a camp. Options can turn this off.
    - Network status and your fire's time left.
    - The rest countdown, your camp buffs and object cooldown.
    - Say and General buttons that post a short message about your camp (General finds your General chat channel, whatever number it has), e.g. "We've placed a camp at [Map Pin Location] (52, 41). Providing Strength and Stamina, plus an anvil and a reagent vendor." It lists the stats the camp's buffs give, then what its higher-tier objects add (up to three). In General, the message ends with " - Happy Camper addon" (in your language). The map pin is the game's own: anyone can click it for a waypoint, with or without Happy Camper. After you leave a camp, the buttons stay ("Share your last camp:") for the last camp you were at, as long as it's still going: its own pin and place go in the message. Camp Builder request announcements carry a pin to the meet-up the same way.
    - Camp pins on the world map and minimap are ringed in the camp's quality, like item colours. With only tier 1 objects a camp is Poor (grey, up to 2 objects) or Common (white, 3 or more). A tier 2 or 3 object makes it Uncommon (green) or better, by points (each object counts its tier): Rare (blue, 8+), Epic (purple, 15+) or Legendary (orange, 25+: five tier 3 objects and five more at tier 2 or better, on a 10-object fire). A camp's tooltip shows its quality and points, each object's tier, and what higher tiers add.
    - At a bare fire (no camp buffs yet) the Camp tab says so. Whoever lit it can still play a song, and anyone there can strum the lute, for roleplay: the music goes out tagged with the fire's rough position and plays only for players within about 30 yards. Nothing else about the fire is sent, and it can't be announced in chat until it has buffs.
    - **My own camp announcement** (Options, At camp): tick it to write the line yourself (Edit wording..., beside the tickbox; the editor opens the first time you tick it, and closing it without saving unticks it again). Up to 120 characters, with blanks Happy Camper fills in when you post: `{where}` (the map pin and coordinates), `{place}` (the place's name), `{offers}` (what the camp gives) and `{time}` (how long the fire has left). The editor's buttons put them in for you, and its preview shows exactly what General would post. Leave out `{where}` and the pin goes at the end. If the line runs long, `{offers}` names fewer things, then the coordinates go. Other braces, like the game's `{skull}`, are left for the game. Say uses the same wording; in General a tickbox in the editor decides whether it ends with " - Happy Camper addon". Your wording is kept for all your characters and in backup codes. (The old Roleplay announcements are gone: players who had them get the short line.)
    - A Find a camp button.
    - The other Happy Campers at your fire, with class, level and professions (as icons with skill ranks; hover the row for names). **Inspect** opens their gear, once the game knows who they are: click on them first, or have them in your group. **Whisper** opens a whisper to them.
  - **Badges**: challenge coins earned by camping, in bronze, silver and gold. They're shared by all your characters, kept in your saved data and backup codes, and never sent to anyone. Earning one says so in chat and drops the coin onto your screen with a glow and a sound (Options can turn the coin off); click an earned coin on the tab to see it again. Unearned coins are dark outlines with no name; hover one for what its next tier asks. "Resting" means a full minute at a fire: a rest a fight breaks off counts for nothing.
    - **Zones**: one for every outdoor zone. Bronze for resting at a fire there, silver for resting at 15 different camps there (yours or anyone's), gold for 50.
    - **Professions**: one for each profession with camp objects, for placing them. Bronze for one, silver for 25 (5 of them tier 2 or better), gold for 100 (20 at its top tier). Cooking's campfires count for Cooking.
    - **Classes** (coming with their art): one for each class, for resting at a fire as that class at levels 15, 40 and 60.
    - **Collections**: Explorer for 10, 25 and 50 zone and profession badges, then Trailblazer, Pathfinder and Summit for every zone and profession badge in bronze, silver or gold (Zephras Isle only needs bronze, since players can't go back there). Hidden until you earn the first; there's a little extra waiting at the top.
    - **Landmarks**: hidden badges for resting at a fire in special places. The section appears once you find one, counted on its own, and they don't count toward the collections.
  - **Music**: your collection of found songs, in both modes (`/hc music`). The title counts the songs you've found ("Songs found: 3 of 10"). A found song shows its title in gold, where you found it (the place and zone, as the game names them) and the day, and **Listen**, which plays it once just for you, at a camp (nothing is sent; it reads **Stop** while it plays, and a song plays out if you walk away). Away from a camp it says "Go to a camp to play this song." A camp song you're hearing is muted while you listen and comes back after; one that starts meanwhile waits. Songs not found yet are greyed out, with the zone they're somewhere in; their titles hint at the place.
  - **Handbook**: every camp object and campfire kit in one place, in both modes (`/hc handbook`). The professions are down the left, then **Campfires**. A profession's page says what every tier of its objects gives at your level and which class buff gives the same stat (they don't stack), then has a card for each tier: what its extras let you do (an anvil, repairs, a trap...), the skill its recipe needs and where it's learned (a trainer, a Camping 101 quest, or the recipe item that teaches it, which you can hover), who drops that recipe item and where (from Wowhead's loot data, named by the game in your language once it has seen them; "not known yet" for the ones nobody has found), its materials (hover one for the item), any tool it needs that isn't used up, how many one craft makes, and your status: **In your bags**, **You can make it**, **No recipe yet**, or, until you've opened that profession's window once, a reminder to. Herbalism, Mining and Skinning say their objects are made in the Gardening, Smelting and Tanning windows. Campfires lists the Basic, Journeyman and Expert kits (3, 5 and 10 objects, and the Cooking skill each needs to place), the Cozy Sleeping Bag, and the camp's rules. Item names come from the game, in your language. Nothing is sent.
  - **Camp Builder**: ask for a camp, laid out like the Group tab: the fire as a figure at the top (slots taken, of how many it holds, with the fire line under it), then a panel with one row per camp buff (a mark for where it stands, its icon, the buff and its profession, **Request** and **Camp Setup** as a pill; rows you can neither bring nor give are dimmed), then a meet-up point (where you're standing, or your map pin).
    - **Camp Setup** fills in what you're bringing from your bags: one camp object (they all share an hour's cooldown, so each player places one) and your campfire kit (the Campfire row). Change it from the row's menu: Nobody, or You (has tier N / can make tier N). Something you bring can't also be asked for, and helpers see what you bring. The class buffs you give show there in blue (Arcane Intellect; a paladin's blessings, one per player).
    - **Melee**, **Caster** and **Balanced** tick a set of buffs: as many objects as the fire holds (a bigger fire you're asking for counts), leaving out what you bring or give, then the rest of the set as class buffs, which take no slot. A line above the rows counts it up: "Fire holds 5 objects: you're asking for 3 and bringing 1.", or with class buffs asked for, "Fire holds 5 objects: asking for 3 and bringing 1. Class buffs asked for: 2." You can't request more objects than the fire has free slots for; to ask for more, request a bigger campfire. With the slots all asked for, ticking a buff that has a class buff asks for that class buff. Buffs your class already gives can still be requested, at any tier: others may want to bring them, or a tier's workspace or service.
    - Each buff you **Request** has a menu of its items by name and tier, e.g. **Mana Well (T1+)**, **Fermenter (T2+)**, **Alchemy Laboratory (T3)** (the lowest tier you'll take); hover the menu or a choice for what that item does: its buff at your level, then the game's own description of it. Higher tiers add a workspace or service, like an anvil or repairs; for a bigger fire, T2 is a Journeyman fire (holds 5) and T3 an Expert fire (10). Only players who can bring that tier, from their bags or the recipes they know, see the request, and each sign-up shows the tier they'll bring.
    - **Class buffs**: a buff that a class buff also gives has one more choice in its menu, e.g. **Blessing of Might (paladin)**, **Power Word: Fortitude (priest)**, **Divine Spirit (priest)**, **Mark of the Wild (druid)**, **Moonkin Aura (druid, needs a party)**, **Arcane Intellect (mage)**, **Strength of Earth Totem (shaman, needs a party)**, **Blessing of Wisdom** and **Blessing of Kings (paladin)**. A class buff takes no slot at the fire, and only players who can give it are offered it. Its tooltip describes it and says if it's a talent (only players who have it can give it: Divine Spirit, Moonkin Aura, Blessing of Kings) or needs a party (Moonkin Aura and Strength of Earth Totem reach only the giver's party). A T1+ object request can also be met by a class buff for the same stat; T2+ and T3 need the object, for its workspace or service. The chat message asks for the class ("Seeking a priest...").
    - Players in the same zone who can bring those objects (one in their bags, or its recipe learned) or give those class buffs see it in their Camp Builder list (which only shows requests in your zone that want something you can bring or give), get a heads-up in their chat frame (naming the class buff when that's what they can give; at most one per requester every 10 minutes, and 3 a minute overall) and can sign up. **Join** asks which when there's a choice: one camp object at most, with your class buffs, and one blessing at most (a paladin gives each player one). Each player has one open request; a new one replaces the old.
    - Once posted, the same rows show how the camp will look: who has signed up for each buff and at what tier (green), who gives it with a class buff ("Bram Stone: Blessing of Might", blue), what you bring, and what's still open, with the meet-up, time left and "Fire holds 5 objects: 3 coming, 1 still open." (with class buffs: "... 3 coming, 1 still open; class buffs: 1 coming, 1 open."). Sign-ups get a waypoint. In the list, each request shows its buffs as small icons (a class buff by its own icon): lit once someone's bringing or giving it, faded while open, ringed in gold where you could bring or give one, with how many are covered. The list scrolls. Each request shows who posted it and how far away it is, and has **Whisper** (opens a whisper to the requester in your chat box, sent only when you press Enter), **Map** (a waypoint on the meet-up, and the map opened on it; outside combat) and **Join**/**Leave** (Leave withdraws everything you signed up with). When someone signs up you get a line in chat, a soft chime and a glowing minimap button (until you open the Camp Builder); no raid warning. Only a player's first sign-up to your request notifies you. If the requester moves the meet-up, sign-ups get a line in chat, and their waypoint follows it (unless they've put it somewhere else since).
    - Older versions of Happy Camper see the objects of a request but not its class buffs, skip a request that wants only class buffs, and don't see class-buff sign-ups; for an older version's request, only objects are offered.
    - Posting needs the camp network on (Options): with it off nobody would see the request, so **Post request** is greyed out (hover it for why). The meet-up has to be on a zone map, not a continent's or a dungeon's.
    - The request closes when a fire is lit at the meet-up, or after 30 minutes.
  - **Ready checks**: at a ready check, Happy Camper tells you (only you, in your chat) who in your group has no Camp Benefits, whose ends within 10 minutes, and whose can't be seen. Each member's is read from their buffs, or from what their Happy Camper last told the group; nothing is sent. Options → Alerts → Camp buffs at ready checks.
  - **Group**: plan a camp with your party or raid. Everyone in the group with Happy Camper shares, over the party or raid addon channel (not the camp channel, and even with the camp network off), every camp object in their bags, when they can place one again, what they placed last and their biggest campfire kit.
    - **At the top**, in a group: **Planned** (objects asked for or already at the fire, of how many it holds, with who has the biggest kit; hover for them all), **Placed** (camp buffs already at the fire, and the latest the group placed) and **Camp Benefits** (how many of the group have it, and who's missing it or whose ends soon; hover for everyone's time left).
    - **Your job**: when the leader asks you to place your camp object, a gold banner says what and who asked, with a **Place** button that uses it (out of a fight; it isn't there in one). Your row in the plan is lit gold and says **You**. It goes once the object is placed.
    - **Camp buffs**: one row per buff, in a fixed order: a mark for where it stands (a green tick: placed; green dot: whoever was asked can place it; orange: they're on cooldown or would have to make it; grey: they don't have Happy Camper; gold ?: still to decide), the object's icon, the buff and its profession, Who has it and Camp Setup. Rows with nothing left to decide (placed, covered by a class buff, or nobody can) are dimmed. **Who has it** lists who carries its object (white) and who could make one (grey), best first. Hover a row for its value at your level and, when the group's levels make it differ, the range across the group ("Your group: +6 Strength (level 20) to +34 Strength (level 60)").

Two more safeguards:
  - **Backup code** (Options -> Backup, or `/hc backup`): your badges, found songs, settings, own chat lines, Find a camp picks and blocked players as one line of plain text. - **When WoW Forever launches** (4 November 2026): if you camped with Happy Camper in the 21 October stress test (its saved data is already in the live game's folder), or in the beta and copied its `HappyCamper.lua` into the live game's `WTF\Account\<account>\SavedVariables` folder (the live account folder's name can differ from the beta's), your first login on the live game asks whether to keep your beta badges and camp progress or start fresh. ## Install

Run `.\deploy.ps1` in PowerShell. It links this folder into the Forever client's `Interface\AddOns` as `HappyCamper`, so edits show up in-game after a `/reload`. It also links the dev tools (next section) unless you add `-NoDev`.

## Dev tools (not for release)

The test and design tools are a separate addon, **Happy Camper (dev tools)**, in the `HappyCamper_Dev` folder next to this one. Happy Camper works the same without it: turn it off in the AddOns list, and leave the folder out of any upload. Any test camps it made are dropped when it's off.

- `/hc broadcast`: an announcement to every Happy Camper player: one of the prepared messages (each player sees it in their own language, its blanks filled in) or free text (shown as written), ending in 1 hour, 6 hours, 24 hours or 3 days. It previews the line in every language Happy Camper speaks, with the message's size (free text longer than one message is refused), and sends only on Send and a second click to confirm. Only the developer's characters can send one: on any other it refuses. While you're online it goes again every 15 minutes or so until it ends (not after the game has blocked one of Happy Camper's actions); the list of what was sent has Stop repeating. Also under Broadcast in `/hc dev`. To add a prepared message: a line in `Broadcast.TEMPLATES` (Broadcast.lua) and its translation in each Locales file.
- `/hc gallery`: Forever's built-in window styles and background art; click a background to try it on the Happy Camper window.
- `/hc have`: what Happy Camper thinks you have, a row per profession: your skill, the camp items in your bags, the camp recipes it remembers, and exactly what each offers you: the Camp Builder's claim (campfire kits count for Cooking) and the Group tab's (camp objects only; the kit has its own line there), flagged where they differ. **Re-check recipes** reads your own open profession window again; **Forget remembered recipes** clears them (click twice, out of a group: your group would hear you know none).

- `/hc memory`, `/hc errors`, `/hc reset guide|panel|recipes|comms` and `/hc state`: memory use and the saved logs' sizes, Happy Camper's recent errors, starting those over, and a copyable dump of settings and this character's data for bug reports. ## Camp auras seen in the beta

- **Campfire Nearby**: a fire is close. It doesn't count as resting.
- **Welcoming Campfire**: you're sitting at a camp. It counts down 60 seconds, and the rest bar fills smoothly along with it, then flashes gold and says "Rested!" for a few seconds when it finishes.
- **Camp Benefits**: the camp's combined stat boosts, applied when that countdown ends.

If camp detection is ever wrong, the dev tools' `/hc learn`, `/hc add` and `/hc forget` help check and correct it. Type `/hc help` for all commands.

## Blizzard rules

The addon follows Blizzard's [UI Add-On Development Policy](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/ui-add-on-development-policy/24534) and the Midnight combat rules that Forever also uses:

- It's free, with plain, readable code, and has no ads or donation requests. Its chat messages never mention the addon.
- **It never sends chat other players can read unless you click a button.** Say and General messages are limited to one every few seconds.
- **It never invites anyone by itself.** A layer invite request (opt-in) only shows a window; the invite is sent when you click Invite.
- Hidden network traffic is kept small:
  - Messages go out at most one per second.
  - It only sends a message when something changes. While you're at a camp, it also sends one presence update a minute.
  - The "what's the latest version?" question goes once a session and every 3 hours after, and gets one answer. Each player's questions are answered at most every 10 minutes, and nothing is asked or answered once the game has blocked one of Happy Camper's actions.
  - The developer's announcements go again at most every 15 minutes or so, only from the developer's own copy, until they end.
  - A zone's camps are asked for at most once every 10 minutes per zone, never in an instance. Only players in that zone answer, each at most every 30 seconds (and each asker at most once a minute), with at most 20 camps; a camp someone else has just sent isn't sent again.
  - Nothing is sent while the game has chat locked down.
- It never acts for you. It doesn't cast spells, place objects or move your character. The cinematic camera only moves the camera, like an AFK screen.
- It doesn't read combat data. It stops reading buffs during combat, and the cinematic camera stops before combat lockdown starts.
- Messages from other players are treated as untrusted. They're validated and cleaned before they're shown. Only whoever reported a camp or request can move, change or remove it. Each player can add one new camp and one new request every 10 minutes (updates aren't limited), and has one lit fire and one open request at a time; a new fire or request in place of their last one only waits 30 seconds.
- What other players can report is limited, so nobody can mess with camps from afar:
  - Objects and Camp Benefits lists are only taken from players at that camp (or its fire-lighter), and only for real campsite objects.
  - Place names travel as the game's area ID, so every name on your map comes from your own game, never from another player's text.
  - Camps and meet-ups are only accepted on outdoor zone maps, never in dungeons.
  - A request only closes for a fire lit by the requester or someone who signed up.
  - Presence says which camp you're at, never where you're standing.
  - A camp passed on by a player in your zone is only taken just after you've asked about that zone, only if you don't know it already (never one of yours, and never one its creator said is gone), and never changes a camp you know.
  - Each player's "send me everything" query is answered at most every 2 minutes, and everyone together can add at most 20 new camps and 10 requests a minute.
- A newer version is only believed when two players report it, or one of the developer's characters does, and at most one major version ahead. Announcements are only taken from the developer's characters, and their text is shown as plain text (no links, colours or pictures).
- `/hc block <name>` hides a player's camps, requests and presence and ignores their messages (`/hc unblock <name>`, `/hc blocked`). Players on your in-game ignore list are treated the same way.

## Limits

- Camps only appear if someone near them runs the addon, and only once someone with the addon has got camp buffs from the fire or placed an object at it. Walking past a fire, lighting one or resting at a bare one doesn't put anything on the map. If you light the fire yourself, its exact burn time waits on your character until you've rested there; then it goes on the map as your camp, taking the place of any report of the same fire. If the fire's owner doesn't have the addon, the first addon user to rest there reports it, but its time left is unknown.
- A camp's buffs come from its objects' professions, which are recognised by the object names in the table above. An object with a name not in the table is only recognised if the game's recipe data links it to a profession.
- The hidden channel (`HappyCamper`) uses one of your numbered chat channel slots, and is listed in your chat tabs' channel settings like any channel you've joined. Nothing from it shows in chat: its messages and notices are hidden, including joins and replies to `/owner` or `/password` (the dev tools' `/hc notices` shows them). Camping Kit's channel is hidden the same way. Untick Share camps with other players (Options → Sharing) to leave HappyCamper, and Join Camping Kit's channel to leave CampingKitNet.
- If the game ever blocks one of Happy Camper's actions, it stops joining, leaving and reordering chat channels for the rest of the session, says so once in chat, and includes it in a bug report's recent errors.
- With the cinematic camera on, the Campfire Circle is hidden too until you move or press a key.
